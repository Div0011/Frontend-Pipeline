# Pattern Analysis: Kieran Clarke — Physical Product / Mechanical Disassembly

## Genre: Consumer Product — Mechanical Disassembly On Scroll (Genre 5)

## Signature Motion
- **Literal physical product exploding into component parts** as camera moves through scroll sequence
- Each part separates with physics-like motion (exponential easing, slight rotation)
- Parts hover in exploded position until user scrolls further

## Mechanical Patterns

### 1. Scroll-Driven Disassembly
- Product enters assembled, at rest
- First scroll: product rotates to show side
- Second scroll: first component separates (exponential ease, 300ms drift)
- Third scroll: second component separates
- By end: all parts floating in exploded view, labels pointing to each

### 2. Camera Choreography
- Camera starts close, pulls back during disassembly
- Orbit path: front → side → 3/4 → top → orbit around during exploded view
- Camera movement uses `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` (slow-in, fast-out)
- When all parts exploded, camera orbits slowly to let user inspect

### 3. Part Highlighting
- Active part (currently separating) has emissive glow
- Previous parts dim to 50% opacity
- Next part to separate pulses subtly (waiting state)

### 4. Spec Label System
- Labels appear at 75% of part's travel distance (not immediately)
- Labels are HTML overlay positioned via 3D-to-screen projection
- Each label has: part name, material, weight, manufacturing detail
- Labels fade in with translateY 10px

## Why It Works
- Proves engineering quality by literally showing what's inside
- Camera choreography creates premium unboxing feel
- Labels educate while entertaining

## Extraction For Template 5
- 3D product model with segmented parts (glTF with separate meshes)
- Scroll-driven separation with exponential easing
- Camera orbit path choreography
- Part highlighting (active glows, previous dims)
- 3D-to-screen label projection for specs
- GlTF model preparation guide (segmentation requirements)
